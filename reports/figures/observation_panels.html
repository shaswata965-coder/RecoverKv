Gradual evolution

obs_tier_dynamics.pdf

Storage moves track future use. Promoted windows draw 2.34× the candidate-set average over the next 32 steps; evicted windows draw 0.66×.

MeasuredA.V, 59 routing events at a 32-step horizon. Lift is a window's future attention over the mean of the windows that eviction decided on; the dashed line at 1× is that average. The legend names each bar and the tier the window ends in, in the method figures' notation: fp16 blue, int2 violet, light for a window that moved into the tier and dark for one that stayed; evicted is a dashed empty box. Whiskers are 95% CIs. Stay low's, 0.94–0.95, is narrower than the whisker's stroke and closes into a single tick: it averages 391,147 windows, 92% of the candidates (arithmetic).

(c)

Query-time concentration

obs_gate_recall.pdf

A few windows carry most of a query's low-bit attention. Each decode step reads only 7 of the 26 low-bit windows; picked by the card gate, those 7 hold 59.2% of the low-bit attention: 93.9% of what an oracle picking with the true attention gets (63.1%), and 2.2× a random 7 (26.9%).

MeasuredA.IV, per query head, head-steps with at least 1% of their attention in the low-bit tier. Why 7 of 26: the gate ratio is 0.25 (the shipped operating point) and this run holds 26 int2 windows per KV head, so each step opens ⌈0.25 × 26⌉ = 7; the other 19 are not dropped, they reach the output through their card's estimate. All three bars read 7 windows and differ only in which: Random is 7 windows drawn blindly, without looking at the query or the attention: each window then has a 7-in-26 chance of being read, so on average they hold 7/26 = 26.9% (arithmetic, not a measurement, so its bar has no interval); Card gate (ours) is the 7 whose cards score highest for the current query; Oracle is the report's hindsight pick, 7 per KV head chosen with the true attention, the ceiling for any gate of that size. 93.9% is the mean per-trace ratio, so it differs from 59.2 / 63.1 in the last digit. Same windows as the oracle: per query head, the card's top 7 share 81.0% of their windows with the true top 7 (a random 7 shares 7/26 = 26.9%) and include the hottest window 96.4% of the time (report §7; no interval reported). Attention captured weighs each window by its attention, which is why the gate gets 94% of the oracle's attention while matching 81% of its windows.
